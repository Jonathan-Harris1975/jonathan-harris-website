What Machine Learning Actually Means for Business

Every business software vendor now claims their product uses machine learning or AI. Some do. Some use a decision tree and a marketing team. Here is how to tell the difference and what actually matters.

By Jonathan Harris — AI author and host of Turing’s Torch AI Weekly

What machine learning is, briefly

Machine learning is a method of building software that learns from examples rather than following hand-written rules. You feed it labelled data — transactions that were fraudulent or not, customers who churned or did not, parts that failed or did not — and it finds patterns that predict outcomes on new data. The prediction improves as more labelled data is added. That is the core of it.

What it is genuinely good at in business

Fraud detection. Credit scoring. Demand forecasting. Churn prediction. Product recommendation. Predictive maintenance. Document classification. These problems share three features: you have historical data with known outcomes, you can define what a good prediction looks like, and a better prediction has measurable business value. If your problem fits this shape, machine learning is worth serious consideration.

Where it fails business users

It fails when the outcome you care about is not in the training data. It fails when the world has changed since the model was trained and no one has noticed. It fails when the prediction is excellent and the action it implies is politically unacceptable. It fails when there is not enough data to find patterns that generalise. It fails when the deployment team treats the model as a black box they do not own and therefore do not monitor.

The questions you need to answer first

What are you predicting? What data do you have that captures the past behaviour you want to predict? How will you validate that the model is actually working — not just on test data but in production? Who owns the model after deployment? What happens when it starts performing worse, as all models eventually do? These questions do not require a data science degree. They require someone senior enough to make the decisions they imply.

Questions answered in this article

Do I need a data science team to use machine learning?

Not necessarily. Mature cloud platforms (AWS SageMaker, Google Vertex AI, Azure ML) have reduced the infrastructure burden significantly. The bigger need is for people who understand the business problem clearly, can prepare and validate data, and can own the deployment and monitoring.

How long does a machine learning project take?

A focused proof of concept with clean data can produce results in weeks. A production deployment that is reliable, monitored, and integrated with existing systems typically takes months. The longer projects tend to be the ones where the data preparation work was underestimated.

What is the difference between AI and machine learning?

Machine learning is a subset of AI. AI is the broader field. Most commercial AI products are machine learning systems. When a vendor says AI, ask which specific technique they are using and what data it was trained on.
