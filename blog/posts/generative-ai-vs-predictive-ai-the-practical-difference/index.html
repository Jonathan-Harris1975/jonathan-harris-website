Generative AI vs Predictive AI: The Practical Difference

Two types of AI are in wide deployment and they are frequently confused. Generative AI creates new content. Predictive AI classifies or forecasts existing data. They have different strengths, different failure modes, and different governance requirements.

By Jonathan Harris — AI author and host of Turing’s Torch AI Weekly

What predictive AI does

Predictive AI takes an input and produces a classification, score, or forecast. A spam filter classifies email as spam or not. A credit model scores the probability of default. A demand forecast estimates next quarter's sales. These systems are trained on historical data with known outcomes and evaluated by how accurately their predictions match reality. This is the kind of AI that has been deployed in business and finance for decades.

What generative AI does

Generative AI takes a prompt or input and produces new content: text, images, audio, code. Large language models like GPT-4, Claude, and Gemini are the dominant examples. They are trained on enormous text datasets and learn to generate plausible continuations of any input. The output is not a classification. It is a generation, which means it can be fluent and coherent while being factually wrong.

Where they differ in practice

Predictive AI fails by being wrong — making a bad classification or a poor forecast. You can measure that failure against ground truth. Generative AI fails by hallucinating — producing confident, well-formed text that is factually incorrect. That failure is harder to detect automatically because the output does not come with a confidence score attached to its factual claims. This is why hallucination is the defining governance challenge for generative AI.

Choosing the right tool

If your problem is: classify this, score this, predict this outcome, detect this anomaly — use predictive ML. If your problem is: draft this, summarise this, answer questions about this corpus, generate variations of this — generative AI is the relevant technology. Many real applications combine both: a retrieval system finds relevant documents (predictive/retrieval), a language model synthesises an answer (generative).

Questions answered in this article

Can I use ChatGPT for business forecasting?

Not reliably. LLMs can discuss forecasting concepts and help you build prompts or code for forecasting models. They should not be used as the forecasting model itself, because they are not trained on your data and will hallucinate numbers that sound plausible.

Is RAG generative or predictive AI?

RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) combines both. A retrieval component finds relevant documents or passages from a knowledge base — a predictive/search task. A generative model synthesises an answer using those retrieved documents. The retrieval grounds the generation and reduces hallucination.

Which is safer in regulated industries?

Predictive AI has a longer track record in regulated industries because its outputs are classifiable and auditable. Generative AI in regulated contexts requires careful scoping, human review, and explicit governance around what the model is and is not allowed to produce or decide.
