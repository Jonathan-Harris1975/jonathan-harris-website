What Is Generative AI? A Clear Explanation

Key Insight

Generative AI does not "understand" content the way humans do — it predicts the most statistically likely next token given its training. This distinction matters enormously when evaluating its outputs for reliability and accuracy.

What Is Generative AI?

Definition: Generative AI

Generative AI refers to artificial intelligence systems that produce new content — text, images, audio, video, or code — rather than classifying or predicting from existing data. These systems are trained on large datasets and learn statistical patterns they use to generate novel, original-seeming outputs.

In Simple Terms

Generative AI learns what plausible outputs look like, then creates new ones on demand. Ask it to write an email, generate an image of a cat, or translate a document — it synthesises a response by drawing on everything it learned during training.

Types of Generative AI

Large Language Models (LLMs)

LLMs are trained on enormous text datasets and learn to predict likely next tokens (words or word-parts) in a sequence. This allows them to generate coherent, contextually relevant text for tasks including summarisation, question-answering, translation, coding, and creative writing. Examples: GPT-4 (OpenAI), Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google).

Image Generation Models

Diffusion models and GANs (generative adversarial networks) generate images from text descriptions or from reference images. Systems like DALL-E, Midjourney, and Stable Diffusion have made AI image generation accessible to non-specialists. These tools are explored in Jonathan Harris's book Lights, Camera, Algorithm: AI's Role in Modern Filmmaking.

Audio and Music Generation

Generative audio models can produce realistic speech synthesis, music composition, and sound effects. AI music tools raise significant questions about authorship and copyright. See The AI Music Revolution for a thorough treatment.

Code Generation

LLMs fine-tuned on code repositories can generate, complete, and debug software code. GitHub Copilot and similar tools have measurably accelerated software development workflows.

Generative AI vs Traditional AI — Key Differences

Traditional AI Generative AI
Classifies, detects, or predictsCreates new content
Discriminative — yes/no, category, scoreGenerative — text, image, audio, code
Spam filter, fraud detector, image classifierChatGPT, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion
Output is a label or numberOutput is a new artefact

Limitations and Risks of Generative AI

  • Hallucinations: LLMs can generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect information, presenting it with apparent confidence.
  • Bias: Models reflect biases present in their training data, which can perpetuate or amplify harmful stereotypes.
  • Copyright: Content generated from training on copyrighted material raises unresolved legal questions.
  • Misinformation: Realistic AI-generated text and images can be used to spread disinformation at scale.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is generative AI?

Generative AI refers to AI systems that produce new content — text, images, audio, video, or code — trained on large datasets and using learned patterns to generate novel outputs.

What is a large language model (LLM)?

A large language model is a type of generative AI trained on vast text data. It learns statistical relationships between words to generate fluent, contextually appropriate text. Examples include GPT-4, Claude, and Gemini.

What is the difference between generative AI and traditional AI?

Traditional AI classifies, predicts, or detects patterns. Generative AI creates new content by synthesising learned patterns — producing text, images, and audio that did not exist in its training data.
